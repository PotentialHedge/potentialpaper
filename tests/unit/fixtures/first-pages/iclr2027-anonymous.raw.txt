INTER-TRAJECTORY DEPENDENCE LIMITS
ANSWER COVERAGE IN LOCAL DLLM REPAIR
Anonymous authors
Paper under double-blind review
ABSTRACT
Test-time compute scaling improves large language model performance by allo-
cating more computation during inference. For diffusion large language models
(dLLMs), sequential repair offers a natural instantiation: it iteratively remasks
low-confidence tokens and regenerates them while retaining the surrounding com-
pletion. However, additional repair rounds often yield limited performance gains.
While recent work has studied inference dynamics within individual trajectories,
our focus is inter-trajectory dependence: whether successive repair candidates
explore distinct answer states. We introduce a diagnostic framework that com-
bines answer-level metrics, hyperparameter sweeps, and remasking interventions
to characterize this dependence. We assess pairwise answer agreement, unique-
answer counts, oracle pass@k, and correction/regression rates across four dLLMs
and three verifiable tasks. We find that sequential local repair produces highly
correlated candidates with substantially lower answer coverage than independent
sampling. The evaluated local-remasking variants do not reliably close this gap.
These findings suggest prioritizing trajectory-level diversification when allocat-
ing test-time compute for answer coverage, with local repair used for refinement.
Our study adds an inter-trajectory perspective to the analysis of dLLM inference
dynamics and provides practical guidance for allocating inference compute.
1 INTRODUCTION
Test-time compute scaling is a central approach for improving large language model perfor-
mance (Wang et al., 2023; Cobbe et al., 2021; Lightman et al., 2024; Snell et al., 2024; Muennighoff
et al., 2025). In diffusion large language models (dLLMs) (Austin et al., 2021a; Nie et al., 2025),
extra inference can be spent on trajectory-level resampling or sequential repair. Resampling starts
each candidate from a fresh masked sequence; repair remasks and regenerates part of an existing
completion. Prior work has developed several remasking-based refinement strategies (Wang et al.,
2025; Zhai et al., 2026; Yao, 2026; Hong et al., 2025), while answer-level coverage across successive
local repairs remains less characterized. We study this coverage by comparing candidates from
retained-state repair with independent restart trajectories under the same model, using budget and
generation-schedule controls to interpret the observed differences.
Recent studies describe commitment and uncertainty within individual dLLM trajectories, including
proximity effects, anchor tokens, confusion zones, and irreversible block decisions (Kim et al.,
2026; Guo & Tan, 2026; Chen et al., 2025a; Wang et al., 2026). These phenomena motivate a
complementary question at the candidate-set level: when a completion is repeatedly repaired, how
much does the resulting chain expand the set of answer hypotheses? We refer to this quantity as
inter-trajectory answer coverage across the candidate set.
